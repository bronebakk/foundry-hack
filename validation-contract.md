# validation-contract.md — Keyworker Force-Multiplier (Hackathon)

*The definition of done for the demo. Written in Phase 1, before features. This is the anchor against drift: the contract does not move to match the code. Changing an assertion requires a logged decision in `decisions.md` (and, for anything material, Jakob's say-so).*

*Milestone → assertion mapping lives in `missions.md`, not here. Keep this file pure assertions.*

*A fresh-context validator at each milestone gate exercises the running system as a real user would and records PASS/FAIL with evidence against the relevant assertions below. It does not read implementation history. The one exception is VAL-PROPOSE-001, important enough to warrant a supplementary code-path check.*

---

## CTX — Pre-meeting context synthesis

### VAL-CTX-001: Synthesised brief from a persona's records
**Scenario**: A keyworker opens a (synthetic) young person's profile before a meeting.
**Expected behavior**: A concise brief appears that synthesises the young person's history across the available records into something readable at a glance.
**Evidence required**: Screenshot of the generated brief shown alongside the source records it draws from.

### VAL-CTX-002: Every claim in the brief traces to a source *(anchors Invariant 7 — explainability)*
**Scenario**: The worker clicks or hovers a statement in the synthesised brief.
**Expected behavior**: The originating record or passage is shown; no claim in the brief is unattributable.
**Evidence required**: Screenshot of a brief claim linked back to its specific source passage.

## DRAFT — Drafting & authorship

### VAL-DRAFT-001: AI produces an editable draft, not a committed record
**Scenario**: The worker requests a case-note draft for a meeting just held.
**Expected behavior**: A draft appears in an editable field, clearly marked as a draft and not yet part of the record of truth.
**Evidence required**: Screenshot of the editable draft with a visible "draft" (uncommitted) status.

### VAL-DRAFT-002: Worker edits are preserved; the committed record is attributed to the worker *(anchors Invariant 5 — authorship integrity)*
**Scenario**: The worker edits the AI draft, then commits it.
**Expected behavior**: The committed record reflects the worker's edits and is attributed to the worker, not the AI.
**Evidence required**: Committed record showing the worker's edits and worker authorship (not "generated by AI").

## PROPOSE — The propose-not-act boundary *(core of the demo)*

### VAL-PROPOSE-001: Nothing is committed without explicit human action *(anchors Invariant 1)*
**Scenario**: The AI generates a draft note or follow-up; the worker then takes no action.
**Expected behavior**: Nothing is persisted to the record of truth and nothing is sent. The generation alone changes no committed state.
**Evidence required**: After generation with no human click, the committed view shows no new record. *Supplementary code check: confirm there is no code path that persists or sends on generation.*

### VAL-PROPOSE-002: No message is sent autonomously *(anchors Invariant 1)*
**Scenario**: The AI drafts a follow-up message addressed to a young person.
**Expected behavior**: The message remains a draft; sending requires an explicit, separate human send action.
**Evidence required**: Outbound/message log shows no send occurred until the worker explicitly clicked send.

### VAL-PROPOSE-003: Every AI output is framed as a proposal, never a decision
**Scenario**: The worker views any AI output anywhere in the system.
**Expected behavior**: It is presented as a draft/suggestion the worker can accept, edit, or discard — never as a settled system decision.
**Evidence required**: Screenshots across surfaces showing proposal framing and a visible discard/reject option.

## DENY — No "computer says no"

### VAL-DENY-001: The system never issues a denial on its own authority *(anchors Invariant 2)*
**Scenario**: A surface where something cannot currently proceed for a young person (e.g. an option is unavailable).
**Expected behavior**: The system does not display a machine-authored refusal. It surfaces options and context, and any actual refusal is routed to a human who delivers it with a reason and an alternative.
**Evidence required**: Screenshot showing the absence of a machine "no," and the path by which a refusal is handed to a human.

---

## ESCALATE — Safeguarding

### VAL-ESCALATE-001: A risk indicator is surfaced to the worker, not acted upon *(anchors Invariant 6)*
**Scenario**: A synthetic persona's records contain a risk indicator (e.g. a self-harm or exploitation cue).
**Expected behavior**: The system surfaces the indicator to the worker and takes no autonomous action — no auto-notification, no auto-referral.
**Evidence required**: Screenshot of the surfaced flag; confirmation that no automatic action or notification was triggered by the flag alone.

### VAL-ESCALATE-002: Escalation is human-initiated and routed to a human-owned surface *(anchors Invariant 6)*
**Scenario**: The worker decides to escalate a flagged risk.
**Expected behavior**: The worker initiates the escalation, which is routed to a designated human-owned escalation surface. The machine neither initiates nor resolves it.
**Evidence required**: Screenshot of the human-initiated escalation arriving at the human-owned surface.

## GOV — Governance & demo credibility

### VAL-GOV-001: All data is visibly synthetic *(anchors Invariant 3)*
**Scenario**: The validator inspects any persona or record used in the demo.
**Expected behavior**: Every persona carries a clear synthetic/demo marker; no real personal data is present anywhere.
**Evidence required**: Screenshots showing synthetic markers across the personas and records.

### VAL-GOV-002: Open-weight models only, behind a swappable provider *(anchors Invariant 4)*
*Revised 2026-06-03 — see decision D-002. The demo proves open-weight dependence and model portability, not literal on-prem hosting. OpenRouter is a deliberate cost stand-in for self-hosted vLLM; only open-weight models that fit a single H100 are ever used.*
**Scenario**: The validator inspects the inference configuration and exercises the full demo path.
**Expected behavior**: Every inference call targets an open-weight model (`gpt-oss-120b` / `llama-3.3-70b`) through an OpenAI-compatible `InferenceProvider` abstraction. No closed/proprietary model (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, etc.) appears anywhere in the path. Switching to a self-hosted endpoint is a base-URL + key change, with no code change.
**Evidence required**: Config/code showing only open-weight model IDs and the single point where the provider base URL is set; the network path resolves to the open-weight endpoint, never a closed-model API.

### VAL-GOV-003: AI suggestions that inform a decision are logged immutably *(anchors Invariant 1; the briefing's "demoable spine")*
*Added 2026-06-03 — see decision D-003. The briefing makes the immutable decision log the central procurement-clearing proof (propose → log → human-approve); it earns a first-class assertion.*
**Scenario**: An AI output is generated and a worker then acts on it (commits an edited draft, escalates, or discards).
**Expected behavior**: The AI suggestion, the human action taken on it, and the human author are recorded in an append-only log that cannot be silently altered, and the entry is viewable/reproducible after the fact.
**Evidence required**: Screenshot of the decision-log entry for a demo action, showing the original AI proposal, the human disposition, and attribution; demonstration that the log is append-only (no edit/delete path).
