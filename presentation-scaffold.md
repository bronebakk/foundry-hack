# Demo deck — scaffold & build brief

*Hand this to the design agent to build the pitch deck. It pairs with `design-brief.md` (same visual language). The deck **sets up and frames the live product** — the product is the hero; slides are scaffolding for the story, not a substitute for it. Keep it short, sober, and confident.*

---

## Deck-level direction

- **Audience:** hackathon judges, including impact investors. They are judging *trustworthiness, defensibility, and whether this matters* — not feature count.
- **Length:** lean. ~9 core slides + the live demo + a small appendix. Assume a short slot (a few minutes of talking, then the live walkthrough carries the weight). Every slide must earn its place; if it's not load-bearing, cut it to a speaker note.
- **Tone:** the same as the product — calm, credible, humane, accountable. Public-sector/charity sensibility, **not** startup-pitch hype. Quiet confidence.
- **Visual language:** inherit from `design-brief.md` and the app — restrained type, the semantic colour cues (proposal/draft, commit/integrity, risk, options, synthetic), light "Northgate Futures" identity. The deck should look like it belongs to the product.
- **One golden thread:** **"AI proposes. The human disposes."** Open on it, close on it, and let every slide be downstream of it.

### Three things to get right (the brief's explicit asks)
1. **Principles lead.** Slide 1 *is* the underlying principles from the original briefing — not a logo/tagline title card. The philosophy is the opening argument; everything else is consequence.
2. **The "parallel hackathon" — wear it with a wink.** The real story: this team **wasn't selected for the official hackathon, so we ran our own** — same brief, same single-day time-box, same constraints, no exemptions — and graded ourselves honestly against them. Frame it as a **tongue-in-cheek, self-aware flex, not a grievance**: gatecrashers who followed every rule on the invitation we didn't get. It disarms (cheeky and confident) *and* it's the strongest credibility signal in the deck — *we imposed the discipline on ourselves with nobody watching.* One clear home (Slide 7) + a wry one-liner in the open that Slide 7 pays off. **Never bitter, never defensive, never an apology.**
3. **Ada resonance: obvious but implicit.** The themes a values-led/impact investor cares about — overlooked people and places, inclusive and accessible technology, ethics and safeguarding, non-extractive/sovereign infrastructure, empowering underserved frontline workers — must be **felt throughout**, but **never named**. No slide says "this fits your thesis." Let the substance do it.

## Slide-by-slide scaffold

> Each entry: **purpose · content · emphasis · design intent.** Titles are working titles for the designer to refine.

### 1 — Principles first *(the lead slide — the briefing's underlying principles)*
- **Purpose:** Anchor the whole pitch in its philosophy before any product or problem talk.
- **Content:** A quiet manifesto. The golden line front-and-centre — **"AI proposes. The human disposes."** Beneath it, the honest value proposition — *"We do the paperwork so you can do the youth work"* — and a tight set of the non-negotiable principles the work is built on (human owns every decision; the machine never says "no" to a young person; runs on open, self-hostable models; synthetic data only; every signal explainable; safeguarding stays human-owned).
- **Emphasis:** These are *invariants we refused to trade away*, not features. This slide should feel like a statement of values.
- **Design intent:** Stark, principle-led, confident. Type-driven, minimal. Not a conventional title card. A subtle, wry one-line nod that this was a self-imposed "parallel hackathon" under the same rules can sit here as a quiet subtitle — *set up the joke that Slide 7 pays off.*

### 2 — Who this is for *(the overlooked)*
- **Purpose:** Put the human stakes first — both the worker and the young person.
- **Content:** Frontline keyworkers — charity caseworkers, supported-employment coaches, FE pastoral staff — who re-engage **NEET young people** (16–24, not in education, employment or training). Overstretched, under-tooled, doing unglamorous high-stakes work for people the system tends to miss.
- **Emphasis:** This serves people and places mainstream tech overlooks — *say it through the work, not as a claim.*
- **Design intent:** Human, warm, real. A representative (synthetic) young person can ground it. Avoid stock-photo gloss.

### 3 — The problem
- **Purpose:** The pain, concretely.
- **Content:** Context scattered across systems; hours lost to paperwork instead of youth work; risk signals buried; and the worst failure mode — a vulnerable young person getting an impersonal *"computer says no."* Time and judgement stolen from where they matter.
- **Emphasis:** The cost is *human capacity and trust*, not just efficiency.
- **Design intent:** Tight, honest. One vivid "before" beat. No fear-mongering.

### 4 — The insight: augmentation, not automation
- **Purpose:** Name what makes this different.
- **Content:** A force-multiplier that does the paperwork and **surfaces** options — while the worker stays the decision-maker of record. The propose-not-act boundary, stated plainly. Drafts in the worker's *own voice*; signals are surfaced for judgement, never acted on.
- **Emphasis:** The discipline (human-in-the-loop by construction) is the product, not a caveat.
- **Design intent:** A single clean diagram of *propose → human disposes → committed*, reused as a motif.

### 5 — Live demo *(the heart — this is where you win)*
- **Purpose:** Show, don't tell. Hand off to the running product.
- **Content:** A minimal "watch this" slide: a small map of the arc so judges can follow — **caseload → AI-synthesised context (with sources) → draft a note in the worker's voice → a safeguarding signal surfaces → the human escalates to a human-owned queue → the immutable decision log.** Then go live.
- **Emphasis:** Out loud during the demo: *nothing is committed, sent, or denied by the machine — every step is a human action; it's running on an open-weight model we host ourselves; not one real person's data.*
- **Design intent:** Almost no text — a visual stepper of the arc, designed to be glanced at, not read. Reuse the app's components so the slide and the product feel continuous.

### 6 — Why you can trust it *(the governance story)*
- **Purpose:** Convert the demo into defensibility.
- **Content:** The invariants made concrete: **synthetic data only** (visible marker); **open-weight, self-hostable** (no closed cloud AI; sovereign by design); **immutable decision log** (every AI suggestion + the human action + the author, append-only); **human-owned safeguarding**; **no "computer says no"**; **explainable** signals traced to source.
- **Emphasis:** This is what makes it *buyable* by a charity, council, or care provider — ethical, accessible, procurement-clearing, non-extractive. (Implicit Ada resonance lives here — let the properties speak.)
- **Design intent:** A calm, scannable "trust ledger." Lean on the decision-log visual as proof.

### 7 — The parallel hackathon *(credibility, with a wink — the explicit ask)*
- **Purpose:** Turn "we weren't selected" into the most disarming *and* most credible slide in the deck.
- **Content:** We didn't get a place at the official hackathon — so we held our own, under the same brief, the same single-day time-box, and the same constraints, and graded ourselves against them honestly. Definition-of-done written first; fresh-context validation gates at every milestone; the invariants never traded away under pressure; a narrow thing that *actually works*, built honestly. The wink lands the charm; the discipline lands the point.
- **Candidate one-liners (designer/presenter picks one — keep it light):** *"We didn't make the guest list. So we ran the party next door — by the same rules."* · *"Uninvited. Undeterred. Same brief, same clock, no exemptions."* · *"Nobody made us follow the rules. That's rather the point."*
- **Emphasis:** Self-imposed rigour *with nobody watching* is the strongest possible signal of how this team builds. Playful on the surface, serious underneath. Confidence, never grievance.
- **Design intent:** Crisp and proud, a knowing wink. Could show the principle-checklist all green, or a light "uninvited / undeterred" motif. One well-delivered line beats a wall of process.

### 8 — Why it matters *(the vision)*
- **Purpose:** Zoom out to the stakes and the scale.
- **Content:** Give overstretched frontline workers their time and judgement back, for the young people most often overlooked. Runs on infrastructure any charity, college, or council can own and afford. A template for **human-owned AI in high-stakes care** — where the human, not the model, is always accountable.
- **Emphasis:** Inclusive, accessible, sovereign by design — a model for AI that empowers rather than extracts. (Implicit resonance, never named.)
- **Design intent:** Aspirational but grounded. One strong forward image.

### 9 — Close / the ask
- **Purpose:** Land it and say what you want.
- **Content:** The specific ask (validation, the conversation, a pilot, next steps). Close by returning to the golden line — **AI proposes; the human disposes** — so the deck ends where it began.
- **Design intent:** Quiet, confident, memorable. Mirror Slide 1.

### Appendix (optional, for Q&A — build only if cheap)
- **A1 — How it runs:** the open-weight stack, sovereign/self-hostable, the base-URL swap to on-prem; low-bandwidth/Chromebook-grade client.
- **A2 — The decision log as procurement proof:** append-only, immutable, fully auditable.
- **A3 — Roadmap / what's next** beyond the demo.

---

## Guardrails for the designer (do / don't)

- **Do** keep the product the hero; slides frame, they don't replace the live walkthrough.
- **Do** make the principles slide the genuine opener and the close echo it.
- **Do** let the Ada-aligned values (overlooked populations, inclusion, accessibility, ethics, sovereignty, frontline empowerment) come through in *substance*.
- **Don't** name the fund, "your thesis," or "your themes" anywhere — implicit only.
- **Don't** turn the parallel-hackathon framing into a grievance, an apology, or a process lecture — it's a confident wink, paid off in one slide. (We chose this challenge; nobody assigned it.)
- **Don't** drift into hype, jargon, or startup-pitch gloss; match the product's sober, humane tone.
- **Don't** overload slides — if it's not load-bearing, it's a speaker note.

---

*North star for the deck: a judge should leave able to repeat one sentence — "the AI does the paperwork, the human always decides" — and trust that the team built it that way on purpose.*
