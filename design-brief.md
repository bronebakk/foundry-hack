# Design brief — Keyworker Force-Multiplier (UX refresh)

*Hand this whole file to the design agent. It is self-contained. Goal: lift the craft and clarity of the UI **without** weakening the governance story that is the product's actual selling point. Read "Non-negotiables" before proposing anything — they are hard constraints, not preferences.*

---

## 1. What this is, in one breath

An **AI augmentation layer for frontline keyworkers** who re-engage NEET young people (16–24 not in education, employment or training) — charity caseworkers, supported-employment coaches, FE pastoral staff. It synthesises a young person's fragmented history into pre-meeting context, drafts case notes and follow-ups *in the worker's own voice*, and surfaces risk/follow-up signals — **always as editable proposals the human authors and commits, never as autonomous actions.**

The promise everything serves: **"we do the paperwork so you can do the youth work."**

This is a hackathon demo (ADA Ventures involved). The audience is **investors/judges first**, and the represented end-users second. The win condition is *credibility* — a judge should grasp the core insight (**AI proposes, the human disposes**, on sovereign open-weight infra, with zero real personal data) within ~60 seconds.

## 2. Why we're engaging design

The current UI is deliberately plain (Pico.css classless defaults) — that sobriety is a feature, not a placeholder. **We are not chasing consumer-SaaS gloss.** We want to keep the sober, trustworthy, public-sector/charity feel while improving: visual hierarchy, the legibility of the propose→dispose moment, flow through the narrative, the decision-log centrepiece, safeguarding tone, accessibility, and responsive behaviour. Think *"the most quietly excellent gov/charity tool you've ever seen,"* not *"a slick startup dashboard."*

## 3. Audience & tone

- **Primary viewers:** impact investors / judges. They are evaluating *trustworthiness and defensibility*, not feature count.
- **Represented users:** time-poor frontline workers, often on **Chromebook-grade hardware over low bandwidth**, doing emotionally heavy work (safeguarding, refusals). Many are non-technical.
- **Tone:** warm, calm, human, accountable. Never alarmist on risk. Never cold or bureaucratic on a refusal. Never "tech-bro." The interface should feel like it *respects* both the worker and the young person.

## 4. Non-negotiables (derived from the product's Hard Invariants — do not break these)

These are the spine of the pitch. A beautiful design that erodes any of them is a failed design.

1. **AI proposes, human disposes — and it must be *visually obvious*.** Every AI output must read unmistakably as an **uncommitted, editable draft**, with the human action (Commit / Send / Escalate / Discard) as the deliberate next step. No AI output may look like a settled system decision. This legibility is the single most important thing to get right.
2. **The worker is the author of record; the AI is a drafting assistant.** Attribution must be clear and never say "generated by AI" as the record's author. Preserve the worker's-voice framing.
3. **No "computer says no."** Nowhere may the system appear to *deny* anything to (or about) a young person. Where an option can't proceed, the UI surfaces **options + context + a human-delivered path** — a person owns the refusal, with a reason and an alternative. Design this as a first-class, warm pattern, not an error state.
4. **Safeguarding is human-owned.** Risk indicators are **surfaced for judgement, never acted on automatically.** Escalation is **human-initiated** and lands in a **human-owned queue** that the machine never marks "resolved." Tone: serious but calm, never red-alert theatrics.
5. **Explainability.** Every surfaced signal/claim traces to its **source record**. Source attribution is a core component to design well (today it's a quote + record id), not something to hide for cleanliness.
6. **Synthetic data, always — and visibly.** A persistent "synthetic demo data" marker must remain present and legible (it's both an ethical line and a trust asset). Make it tasteful, but keep it unmissable.
7. **Open-weight / sovereign.** The product runs on a self-hostable open-weight model. The decision log already exposes this provenance; keep it inspectable and legible. Don't imply a closed cloud AI.

If a proposed design improves aesthetics but blurs #1, #3, or #4, **flag it and choose the invariant.**

## 5. Current state — surfaces to redesign

Server-rendered app; six surfaces forming one narrative arc. (Screenshots provided separately — they are the canonical "before".)

1. **Caseload** (`/`) — grid of synthetic young people as cards; safeguarding signals tagged. The entry point.
2. **Context** (`/context/{id}`) — an AI-synthesised pre-meeting brief, with every claim traceable to its source record. The "walk in prepared" moment.
3. **Drafting** (`/drafting/{id}`) — worker generates a case note or follow-up; an **editable draft** appears inside a dashed "AI proposal — DRAFT, not committed, nothing sent" frame with **Commit / Discard** (and Send for follow-ups); plus a ledger of what's been committed/sent. **This is the hero surface — the propose/dispose moment.**
4. **Safeguarding** (`/escalation/{id}`) — a risk flag (e.g. exploitation, low-mood/self-harm) surfaced with its **source quote + "why flagged" rationale**, under a "no automatic action has been taken" callout; an editable escalation note the worker may send; and the "no computer says no" **options** section.
5. **Safeguarding inbox** (`/escalation/inbox`) — a **human-owned queue**; each item shows it "arrived because the worker sent it" and is "awaiting human review." The machine resolves nothing.
6. **Decision log** (`/governance/`) — the append-only governance spine: each entry shows **AI proposal → human disposition → worker of record**, with disposition badges, a per-person filter, an "append-only / no edit-delete" banner, and the open-weight inference provenance. **This is the governance centrepiece — judges will linger here.**

### Current visual language (evolve, don't discard)
A semantic colour mapping is already in use and should be *refined*, not replaced:
- **Grey / dashed border** = uncommitted AI proposal (draft)
- **Green** = committed / append-only integrity
- **Red** = safeguarding / risk / escalate (used calmly, not alarmingly)
- **Blue** = options / send / informational
- **Yellow** = the persistent synthetic-data banner

## 6. UX problems & opportunities to address

- **Hierarchy & density:** everything currently sits at one flat visual level (Pico defaults). Establish a clear type scale, spacing rhythm, and emphasis so the eye lands on the right thing (the proposal, the source, the human action).
- **The propose→dispose moment (priority #1):** make the "this is a draft you own and must act on" reading instant and confident. The dashed-box pattern works conceptually — elevate it into a polished, unmistakable component.
- **Flow through the arc:** today, moving between surfaces relies on the global nav. Design a way to carry **one young person** continuously through context → draft → safeguarding → log (e.g. a persistent person header / stepper), so the demo flows as one story.
- **Source-attribution component:** make "claim → its source record" elegant and repeatable (used in Context and Safeguarding). It's central to the explainability claim.
- **Safeguarding tone:** convey seriousness and care without alarm; make the human-owned escalation and inbox read as calm, accountable handoffs.
- **"No computer says no":** turn this into a recognisable, warm pattern (options + alternative + named human), not a generic empty/error state.
- **Decision log readability:** it's the procurement-clearing proof — make the proposal→disposition→author triplet scan beautifully at a glance, with the append-only/immutability cue feeling reassuring.
- **States & micro-copy:** empty states, the HTMX in-place generate/commit transitions (loading/confirmation), button labels, and helper text. Keep copy plain and human.
- **Accessibility:** public-sector context — target **WCAG 2.2 AA** (contrast, focus states, keyboard nav, screen-reader semantics, no colour-only meaning).
- **Responsive:** workers on the move — must work down to a small laptop/tablet; graceful on a Chromebook.
- **Light identity:** the fictional delivery org is **"Northgate Futures."** A restrained, credible visual identity (wordmark, one accent, supportive neutrals) — *light-touch*, not a heavy brand.

## 7. Scope

**In scope:** visual system (type, colour tokens, spacing, components), redesigned layouts for all six surfaces, the hero proposal/dispose component, the decision-log component, source-attribution component, person-arc navigation, states & micro-copy, accessibility, responsive behaviour, light Northgate Futures identity.

**Out of scope / do not propose:** new product features or surfaces; anything that implies autonomous AI action; removing the synthetic banner, source attributions, draft framing, or the "no computer says no" pattern; a heavy front-end rebuild (see constraints).

## 8. Technical constraints (the design must be buildable as-is)

- **Stack:** Python **FastAPI + server-rendered Jinja2 templates + HTMX** for interactivity + **Pico.css (classless)** + **SQLite**. **No SPA framework, no build step**, low client bandwidth, self-hostable/sovereign.
- Deliver designs that are implementable as **semantic HTML + a lightweight CSS layer** (you may extend or replace Pico, but keep total CSS/JS footprint small and dependency-light — no Tailwind/JS build pipeline, no megabyte bundles, minimal/no external fonts; system or a single self-hosted font is ideal).
- Interactions are HTMX partial swaps (generate/commit/escalate). Design the in-place transitions and confirmations accordingly; everything must **degrade gracefully without JS** and **render fast on a Chromebook over poor wifi**.
- Prefer coded **HTML/CSS prototypes** (portable straight into Jinja templates) over tool-locked mockups; if producing mockups, pair them with implementation notes.

## 9. Deliverables

1. **Visual system / mini design-system:** type scale, semantic colour tokens (keep the proposal/commit/risk/options/synthetic mapping), spacing, elevation, and the core components — card, **proposal/draft frame**, disposition **badges**, **source-attribution chip**, inbox item, **decision-log entry**, callouts (safeguarding, no-deny, synthetic).
2. **High-fidelity redesigns of all six surfaces** (coded HTML/CSS preferred), with the **drafting propose/dispose moment** and the **decision log** as the two showcase screens.
3. **The person-arc navigation** concept (carrying one young person through the flow).
4. **Annotated rationale:** brief notes tying key choices back to the non-negotiables and the tone.
5. **Accessibility notes** (contrast/keyboard/SR) and **responsive** behaviour.
6. **Implementation notes:** how it maps onto Pico/Jinja/HTMX with a small footprint.

## 10. Success criteria

- A first-time judge grasps **"AI proposes, the human disposes"** within ~60 seconds, just from looking.
- The UI feels **trustworthy, warm, and accountable** — appropriate to safeguarding and to a charity/public-sector buyer — not like a flashy SaaS dashboard.
- Every non-negotiable in §4 is visibly upheld (point to where).
- It's implementable in the existing stack with a light footprint; nothing requires a heavy rebuild.
- Accessible (WCAG 2.2 AA) and usable on modest hardware/bandwidth.
